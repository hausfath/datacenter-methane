# Marginal emission rate sources (US electricity): short-run and long-run

Compiled 2026-10-26. Purpose: primary sources for rebutting/qualifying the "use marginal, not average" critique of the off-grid-gas vs grid per-MWh comparison (blog: recips ~736, simple-cycle turbines ~705 kg CO2e/MWh; 2023 eGRID averages ~410-430 kg CO2e/MWh for ERCOT, PJM, US).

Verification key. VERIFIED = I read the primary document text myself in this session (file text, spreadsheet cells). SECONDARY/UNVERIFIED = seen only in a search snippet, news article, abstract or third-party summary. DERIVED = my arithmetic on verified numbers. Nothing here is from memory unless flagged.

Unit conversions: 1 lb = 0.45359 kg; 1 lb/kWh = 453.59 kg/MWh; short ton = 907.185 kg. CO2e figures I computed use AR6 GWP100 (CH4 29.8, N2O 273), which is the Cambium workbook's default lookup (GWP sheet). AR5 (28/265) and 20-yr values are available in the same sheet.

Network notes: www.nrel.gov no longer resolves; NREL was renamed National Laboratory of the Rockies (NLR) in Dec 2025 (DOE announcement, https://www.energy.gov/cmei/articles/energy-department-renames-nrel-national-lab-rockies, seen in search results only, UNVERIFIED). Use docs.nlr.gov, data.nlr.gov, www.nlr.gov, scenarioviewer.nlr.gov. cdm.unfccc.int blocks scripted access (Incapsula bot check) and its PDF text could not be extracted in the browser; I did not circumvent it.

## 0. Headline take for the data-center analysis

1. Short-run (operating margin) estimates for 2019-2023 sit well above the grid averages the blog used, for combustion CO2 only:
   - Holland et al. 2019 marginal: Texas 1.085 lb/kWh = 492 kg/MWh; East 1.407 = 638; US 1.303 = 591 (vs their 2019 averages Texas 0.996 = 452, East 0.879 = 399, US 0.862 = 391).
   - AVERT 2023 "Uniform EE" (flat displacement): Texas 587, Mid-Atlantic 618, US weighted 648 kg CO2/MWh.
   - So on a short-run marginal basis, the grid comparator is ~490-650 kg CO2 (combustion only), not 410-430. Adding upstream CH4 (Cambium-style precombustion ~110-140 kg CO2e/MWh for gas-heavy margins) would push it higher. The blog's 705-736 kg CO2e (combustion + upstream at matched leakage) is therefore still above short-run marginal grid, but the gap shrinks from ~+70% to roughly 0 to +20% once an upstream term is added to the marginal grid number (rough arithmetic: Texas 492-587 + ~110-140 upstream = ~600-730; Mid-Atlantic 618 + ~110 = ~730; US 648 + ~110 = ~760, versus 705-736). Whether upstream is counted on both sides matters. This needs a like-for-like calculation (see section 5); the upstream add-ons here are illustrative, not computed.
2. Long-run marginal (Cambium 2024 LRMER, Mid-case) is much lower, and falls steeply after 2025: ERCOT 410 kg CO2e/MWh combustion in 2025, 152 in 2030, 53 in 2035. But Cambium 2024 assumes IRA tax credits do NOT phase out and was built before OBBBA (July 2025). It is therefore not a credible forecast of what is actually being built for data centers (gas-heavy, see section 6). Do not use the 2030+ LRMER values as a defense of grid-served data centers without caveating this.
3. NREL/NLR's own guidance (Gagnon & O'Shaughnessy 2024; Cambium 2024 documentation) is that a SRMER alone is "typically unsuitable" for a comprehensive impact of a new load, and that a long-run (or blended) rate is preferred for long-lived prospective analysis of loads. That supports the critics' point about the average being the wrong metric, but it cuts against using short-run marginal too, and the long-run answer depends on what is actually built.
4. Cambium 2025 does NOT appear to exist as of 2026-10-26 (checks in section 4). So there is no post-OBBBA Cambium LRMER.

### 2.3 Gagnon & O'Shaughnessy 2024 (NREL)  [VERIFIED PDF text]

- Citation: Gagnon, P., O'Shaughnessy, E. (2024). Consequential Analysis of the Greenhouse Gas Emissions Impacts of Actions That Influence the Electric Grid: The Theory and Practice of Using Marginal Emissions Rates. NREL/TP-6A40-91580, December 2024. https://docs.nlr.gov/docs/fy25osti/91580.pdf (also https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy25osti/91580.pdf). Funding note in the report: "Funding provided by Amazon.com Services LLC under agreement number FIA-20-17145." (disclose when citing).
- Short vs long run definitions: "Short-run analysis: Estimates or analysis performed assuming that capital assets are fixed, and therefore impacts are purely operational. Long-run analysis: ... assuming that capital assets can change in response to an action..."
- Recommended approach (Eq. 3): "MER = SRMER x w_t + LRMER x (1 - w_t)", with w_t "a weight designed to reflect the relative influence of the SRMER and LRMER terms, based on how long it is expected to take for the structural response to the intervention to occur." Worked example: 3-year lag and 20-year load, "this would equate to a w_t of 0.15." Also: "To the author's knowledge, no research exists providing guidance on appropriate values for w_t."
- Preference vs build margin: "LRMERs are not equivalent to the build margin ... This is the primary reason why this document recommends this method as preferable over the method described in the following section".
- On new loads: "Some uses of consequential emissions analysis seem, to the authors, to likely be generally accepted as suitable (e.g., long-lived prospective analysis of policies or loads, given its current widespread use for that purpose already)". The example in sec. 2.2 is "a hypothetical load addition in 2024, that will persist for 20 years". The report does not mention data centers by name (grep found none) and makes no explicit ERCOT/PJM statement. It also says "this paper does not make broad concluding statements about the suitability of current methods."
- Implication for a large, long-lived new load: a mostly-LRMER (low w_t) composite is the NREL recommended practice, but the LRMER must come from a model that reflects what is actually built (see 2.1 caveat on pre-OBBBA policy).

### 2.4 UNFCCC CDM TOOL07 (emission factor for an electricity system)  [PARTIAL: original PDF not retrievable]

- Citation: UNFCCC CDM, TOOL07 "Tool to calculate the emission factor for an electricity system", Version 07.0, https://cdm.unfccc.int/methodologies/PAmethodologies/tools/am-tool-07-v7.0.pdf . Version: Verra's draft states "TOOL07, v7.0 was adopted on 31 August 2018" and "v1.0 of this tool was adopted on 19 October 2007" (VERIFIED, Verra M0325 draft). A search snippet describes v7.0 as the CDM revision for small isolated grids; whether v7.0 is still the latest on the UNFCCC site is UNVERIFIED (a Verra consultation, Oct 2024, still references v7.0 as "most recent").
- Original TOOL07 text (combined-margin weights wOM/wBM defaults, BM 10-year age rule, OM options) could not be read: UNFCCC site returns an Incapsula bot check to scripted fetches and the browser PDF view had no extractable text. I do NOT give the original default weights from memory. UNVERIFIED.
- What IS verified (Verra draft "M0325 Electricity System Emission Factor", draft v1.0, 3 Oct 2024, https://verra.org/documents/m0325-electricity-system-emission-factor/ , which states it replaces specific TOOL07 paragraphs): 
  - Definitions: "Build margin (BM) emission factor: The emission factor representing future power plants in the electricity system whose construction and subsequent dispatch and generation would be displaced or affected by the project activity." "Combined margin (CM) emission factor: The weighted average of the operating margin and build margin emission factors..."
  - BM sample group (para. 75, same as TOOL07 steps a-c): "(a) Identify the set of five power units that started to supply electricity to the grid most recently (SET5 units)... (b) Identify the set of power units that started to supply electricity to the grid most recently and that comprise 20 percent of the total annual electricity generation ... (c) From SET5 units and SET>=20 per cent select the set of power units that comprises the larger annual electricity generation (SET sample)." (Verra notes it ignores TOOL07 steps d-f, i.e. the capacity-addition/age conditions, whose content I could not read.)
  - Verra's replacement weights (a DRAFT VCS revision, not TOOL07): projects supplying/saving grid electricity: wind/solar wOM=0.50/wBM=0.50 (first crediting period), 0.40/0.60 (second), 0.30/0.70 (third); other projects 0.40/0.60 then 0.25/0.75. Case 2, "projects increasing the consumption of electricity from the grid": "For all crediting periods: wOM = 1.0 and wBM = 0.0". Imports: "1.3 t CO2/MWh for projects increasing the consumption of electricity from the grid". Relevance: for a load-adding project, the CDM-lineage convention puts all weight on the OM (an average or operating margin), which supports a short-run view for loads; but this is a draft Verra rule, not CDM TOOL07 as such.
  - A default BM of "0.326 tCO2/MWh may be applied (NG-fired CCGT, based on best available technology)" when renewables <= 20% of installed capacity (para 91, Verra draft).
- GHG Protocol consultation feedback mentions "Default 0.5 build margin weight" and "UNFCCC CDM Tool07" as options for weighting (see 2.5), consistent with a 50/50 default but this is not the TOOL07 text.

## 5. How to put the numbers on the same footing as the blog (suggested, not done)

The blog compares kg CO2e/MWh including upstream CH4 at matched leakage. Marginal sources above differ:
- AVERT, Holland, CEDM: combustion CO2 only, no upstream, so add upstream (at least for the gas share of the margin; Cambium precombustion for gas-heavy regions is ~105-140 kg CO2e/MWh on a ~410 kg combustion margin, e.g. ERCOT 2025: 137 precombustion CO2e vs 410 combustion).
- Cambium LRMER: provides both, kg CO2e/MWh.
- Region definitions are not interchangeable (AVERT Mid-Atlantic != PJM; Holland East != PJM; Cambium PJM_East/PJM_West; CEDM RFC).
- A clean sensitivity table would use: short-run: AVERT 2023 uniform (TX 587, Mid-Atl 618, US 648 kg CO2) + upstream; Holland 2019 (TX 492, East 638, US 591); long-run: Cambium 2024 Mid-case 2025 and 2030 values with an explicit flag that they are pre-OBBBA, and a NREL-style blend with w_t.
