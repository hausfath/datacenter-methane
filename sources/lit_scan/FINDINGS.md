Verification tags: VERIFIED = I opened the page/PDF/abstract and the quote is from it (saved locally unless noted). UNVERIFIED = seen only in a search-result summary/snippet or secondary source. No DOI is given unless it appeared on a page I opened.

## 1. Methane slip, reciprocating engines

**Vendor data sheet, Caterpillar G3516 (1200 rpm, 60 Hz genset, 2.0 g/bhp-hr NOx set point) [VERIFIED, file `cat_dm5150_g3516B.pdf`; URL https://powergenenterprises.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/dm5150-03-e.pdf (dealer-hosted copy of Cat TMI dm5150-03)]**
- Quote: "THC (molecular weight of 15.84) (14) g/bhp-hr 5.32 4.13 4.47" and "NMHC ... 0.8 0.62 0.67" at 100/75/50% load; note 14: "CO, CO2, THC, and NMHC VALUES ARE 'NOT TO EXCEED'". Fuel consumption (nominal) 7049 / 7119 / 7614 BTU/bhp-hr (LHV basis, "FUEL LHV 905 BTU/SCF"); engine efficiency (nominal) 36.1 / 35.7 / 33.4%.
- My arithmetic (CH4 approx THC minus NMHC; 50 MJ/kg CH4 LHV): CH4 4.5 / 3.5 / 3.8 g/bhp-hr = 6.1 / 4.7 / 5.1 g/kWh shaft; about 3.0% / 2.3% / 2.4% of fuel energy-as-CH4 at 100/75/50% load. These are not-to-exceed limits (upper bound), engine-out, and this is an older, higher-NOx model, NOT the G3516H/G3520K now ordered. Part-load values are not monotonic; do not read a strong load trend from one sheet.
- Cat G3520K / Jenbacher J620/J624 THC sheets: NOT obtained (Cat cat.com pages and Jenbacher brochures returned no THC numbers in search). Gap.

**Field measurement, US gas compressor engines [VERIFIED abstract, `osti_1760629.html`; https://www.osti.gov/pages/biblio/1760629]** Vaughn, Luck, Williams, Marchese, Zimmerle, Environ. Sci. Technol. (published Jan 2021; DOI shown on page: 10.1021/acs.est.0c05492).
- "The mean methane emission rate from 63 four-stroke, lean-burn (4SLB) compressor engines was 5.62 kg/h (95% CI = 5.15-6.17 kg/h) and ranged from 0.3 to 12.6 kg/h. The mean ... from 39 four-stroke, rich-burn (4SRB) ... was 0.40 kg/h". "Study results for 4SLB engines were lower than both ... AP-42 and ... GHGI by 8 and 9%". 4SRB = 43% of AP-42 factor.
- Not per kWh or per load in abstract; compressor duty, not genset. Load dependence not in abstract.

**Marine LNG engines, ICCT FUMES (Jan 2024) [VERIFIED, `icct_fumes_report.pdf`/`.txt`; https://theicct.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/ID-64-%E2%80%93-FUMES-ships-Report-A4-60037-FV.pdf (linked from https://theicct.org/publication/fumes-characterizing-methane-emissions-from-lng-fueled-ships-using-drones-helicopters-and-on-board-measurements-jan24/)]**
- LPDF 4-stroke (dual-fuel, Otto cycle): "averaged 6.42% with a median of 6.05%" (22 measurements, 18 ships) of fuel; vs EU default 3.1%, IMO 3.5% (Table 2 in report). Slip highest at lowest loads; 10% load point 10x higher in g/kWh (report line ~202).
- Lean-burn spark-ignited (LBSI, the engine class most similar to land gensets): "two measurements from two unique ships ... methane slip of 2.41% and 1.85% at 36% and 55% main engine load ... lower than the EU and IMO assumption of 2.6% ... we do not have enough data to determine whether the default factor for LBSI engines is reasonable." Defaults table: LBSI 2.6% (IMO and EU). Literature cited in report: Stenersen & Thonstad 2017 LBSI 2.32%; Pavlenko 2020 LBSI 4.1 g/kWh (2.6%); Sphera/Schuller 2021 LBSI 2.00 g/kWh (1.3%).
- Caveat: marine, E2/E3 duty cycles, % is of fuel (mass basis as defined by ICCT); only n=2 for LBSI.

**Marine load dependence / industry counter-view [UNVERIFIED, search summaries only]:** FUMES 6.07% average at >=50% load (Riviera Maritime); Wartsila et al. argue sample dominated by old low-pressure engines at 0-30% load and that slip has fallen ~4x in 20 years (Hellenic Shipping News). One ferry/lean-burn study reporting roughly 2x slip at 25% vs 100% load: seen only in snippet. A separate MDPI paper I did open (J. Mar. Sci. Eng. 13, 1379, `jmse13_01379.pdf`) reports a yearly average Cslip of 1.57% for a RoPax with LPDF 4-stroke engines at high stable load (abstract: "yearly average methane slip coefficient (Cslip) obtained was 1.57%"); it is not the lean-burn load study referenced above.

**Oxidation catalysts [UNVERIFIED, search snippets only]:** NPC topic paper (energy.gov Infra_Topic_Paper_4-9) said to state oxidation catalysts "cannot reduce methane emissions at the low exhaust temperatures typical of legacy engines"; VTT study: methane oxidation catalyst cut CH4 "up to 70-80% at ... 550 degrees", deactivates (sulfur) and needs regeneration. Standard Pt/Pd oxidation catalysts on lean-burn engines are designed for CO/formaldehyde; treat CH4 reduction as ~0 unless a vendor guarantees otherwise. I did not open either source.

**Best values to use (engine):** central ~2-3% of fuel energy as CH4 for modern 4SLB gensets, range ~1.3-3.5% (ICCT-cited marine LBSI 1.3-2.6%; Cat G3516 sheet 2.3-3.0% not-to-exceed; 4SLB compressors above). Mass equivalent ~4-6 g CH4/kWh shaft for the Cat sheet. Run 1% and 4% as sensitivities; add a part-load multiplier sensitivity.
**Gaps:** Jenbacher/Wartsila/MAN land-genset THC sheets; Cat G3520K/G3516H THC; a peer-reviewed land-genset load curve; primary source for catalyst CH4 conversion; Caterpillar/INNIO permit CH4 factors for the actual data center projects; PTAC field-test report (15CERI1.pdf returned HTTP 403).

## 2. Gas turbines and combined cycle

- **AP-42 Section 3.1 (4/00), Table 3.1-2a [VERIFIED, `ap42_3_1.pdf`/`.txt`; https://www.epa.gov/sites/default/files/2020-10/documents/c03s01.pdf]**: natural gas-fired turbines, uncontrolled, "Methane 8.6 E-03 lb/MMBtu (fuel input)", rating C. (= 0.0039 kg/MMBtu, consistent with Table W-7 0.004.) At 7,535 Btu/kWh (CC) this is ~0.029 g CH4/kWh; at 10,975 (GT) ~0.043 g/kWh (my arithmetic). Table 3.1 factors apply to simple and combined cycle (page says lb/MMBtu basis applies to cogeneration/combined cycle).
- **Field, aircraft mass balance, 14 US gas plants [VERIFIED abstract via Europe PMC, DOI on page 10.1021/acs.est.9b01875]**: Hajny et al. 2019: "loss rate ranging from -0.039% to 0.204%", "average ... emission factor (EF) of 5.4 (+10/-5.4) g CH4/MMBTU ... within uncertainty of the EPA EFs. However, one facility measured during startup exhibited ... 440 (+660/-440) g CH4/MMBTU and a loss rate of 2.5%". Startup/part-load can be large.
- **Korea 8 GTCC plants [VERIFIED, `korea_ccgt_pmc.html`; https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3536972/]**: "the emission factor for CH4 stood at '0.82 kg/TJ'", 18% below IPCC 1 kg/TJ.
- Aeroderivative/simple-cycle (LM2500/TM2500/Titan) specific CH4 measurements: none found. Use AP-42 as proxy.
**Best values:** turbines/CC 0.004 kg CH4/MMBtu (about 0.02% of fuel mass as CH4, given ~19 kg CH4 per MMBtu HHV; my arithmetic); negligible vs engines (~100-300x lower), but flag startup-transient (Hajny 440 g/MMBtu) and low-load aeroderivative sensitivity at ~10x AP-42.
**Gaps:** direct measurements on aeroderivatives/mobile turbines; load dependence.

## 3. US operating heat rates

- **EIA Electric Power Annual Table 8.1 [VERIFIED, `eia_t8_1.html`; https://www.eia.gov/electricity/annual/html/epa_08_01.html]**: this table is by FUEL, not prime mover. "Average Operating Heat Rate for Selected Energy Sources, 2015 through 2025 (Btu per Kilowatthour)": natural gas 2025 = 7,781; 2024 = 7,754; 2023 = 7,721 (utility + IPP sectors, "Combined heat and power plants, and all plants in the commercial and industrial sectors are excluded").
- **EIA Table 8.2 [VERIFIED, `eia_t8_2.html`; https://www.eia.gov/electricity/annual/html/epa_08_02.html]**: "Average Tested Heat Rates by Prime Mover and Energy Source, 2015 - 2025", natural gas 2025: Steam Generator 10,293; Gas Turbine 10,975; Internal Combustion 8,853; Combined Cycle 7,535 Btu/kWh. 2024: 10,337 / 10,999 / 8,924 / 7,548. Note: "Heat rate is reported at full load conditions ... weighted by Net Summer Capacity" (tested, full-load, not operating).
- HHV vs LHV: neither page states the basis. EIA convention is HHV (UNVERIFIED on these pages); Cat/Jenbacher sheets are LHV. LHV/HHV for pipeline gas ~0.90 (check). Convert before mixing.
**Best values:** CC 7,535; GT 10,975; ICE 8,853 Btu/kWh (all 2025 tested full-load, likely HHV); fleet gas operating 7,781. Cat G3516 sheet: 7,049-7,614 Btu/bhp-hr LHV at shaft (~9,300-10,000 Btu LHV per kWh elec after 94.6% generator efficiency).
**Gap:** operating (not tested) heat rate by prime mover (would need EIA-923 computation or EPM tables); eia.gov basis statement.

## 4. Upstream and midstream methane loss rates

- **Alvarez et al. 2018 Science [VERIFIED abstract via Europe PMC, DOI on page 10.1126/science.aar7204]**: "13 +/- 2 teragrams per year, equivalent to 2.3% of gross U.S. gas production ... ~60% higher than the U.S. EPA inventory". Denominator: gross production (2015 data).
- **Sherwin et al. 2024 Nature [VERIFIED from the article page `sherwin_2024.html`, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-07117-5, and correction `sherwin_correction.html`, https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-026-10605-5, published 29 May 2026]**: current text: "Total estimated emissions range from 0.71% (95% confidence interval (CI) 0.61%, 0.81%) of covered natural gas production in a high-productivity, gas-rich region to 9.77% (95% CI 9.19%, 10.45%) in a rapidly expanding, oil-focused region. The six-region weighted average is 2.43% (95% CI 2.27%, 2.59%), or roughly three times the national government inventory estimate". Use 2.43%, not 2.95%. Caveats: (a) it is a weighted average over the six surveyed regions ("covered natural gas production"), national extrapolation is a separate step (not seen in abstract); (b) the correction page text lists corrections (overflight counts, midstream double-counting removal, unit-conversion error, sorting error) and says "The main results, conclusions, and our interpretations of the data remain unchanged", but the correction page itself does not print 2.43; the number comes from the corrected article page. The correction's SI Section S15 not opened.
- **Permian:**
  - Zhang et al. 2020 Sci. Adv. [VERIFIED abstract, DOI on page 10.1126/sciadv.aaz5120]: "2.7 +/- 0.5 Tg a-1 ... 3.7% of the gross gas extracted in the Permian" (May 2018-Mar 2019).
  - Chen et al. 2022 ES&T [VERIFIED abstract, DOI 10.1021/acs.est.1c06458]: New Mexico Permian only, "194 (+72/-68, 95% CI) t/h, or 9.4% (+3.5%/-3.3%) of gross gas production" (Oct 2018-Jan 2020). Not the whole basin.
  - ES&T 2026 TROPOMI/IMI inversion [VERIFIED abstract, DOI 10.1021/acs.est.5c08745; authors not retrieved]: Permian 2019-2023 "4.0 +/- 1.1 Tg a-1"; "Methane intensity fell from 5.2 to 3.2% as production surged. Intensity in the New Mexico Permian fell from 4.5 to 2.1%". Also Anadarko, Barnett, Eagle Ford, Haynesville "decreasing emissions and intensities" (no Haynesville number in abstract).
  - Bridger LiDAR 2024 inventory, ES&T 2026 [VERIFIED abstract, DOI 10.1021/acs.est.5c15184]: "Total annual basin emissions were 5,133 kt CH4 ... The basin-wide methane loss rate was 3.13%. Texas ... 3.6% loss rate, while New Mexico ... 2.1%".
  - EDF/MethaneSAT Delaware sub-basin [VERIFIED page text `edf_methanesat_nm.html`, https://www.edf.org/methanesat-observations-reveal-lower-methane-intensity-new-mexicos-permian-basin-associated]: "nine separate observations ... 2024-2025 ... methane intensity in New Mexico is around 1.2%, whereas in Texas it is 3.1%. Methane intensity is the amount of methane emitted compared to the amount of gas that is produced and sold." Advocacy press page, MethaneSAT data not peer reviewed per search summary (UNVERIFIED).
  - Varon et al. 2023 (AMT weekly TROPOMI Permian): NOT verified; Europe PMC did not return it. A different 2023 JGR TROPOMI paper (DOI on page 10.1029/2022jd037479, 3.0 +/- 0.7 Tg/yr 2019) was seen. Gap.
- **Appalachia/Marcellus:** Appalachian Methane Initiative 2026 report via EDF blog [VERIFIED blog text `edf_appalachia.html`, https://blogs.edf.org/energyexchange/2026/05/28/low-producing-conventional-wells-high-loss-rates-drag-down-appalachias-energy-industry/; AMI report itself not opened]: "AMI estimates that the region's methane loss rate is 0.52% of natural gas production (95% CI: 0.30-0.62%), similar to an analysis of MethaneSAT data collected between 2024 and 2025 which found a loss rate of 0.6%." Conventional wells 18.3%, unconventional 0.09% (search summary, UNVERIFIED in detail). 2023 tower-based 0.30-0.45% for Marcellus: UNVERIFIED.
- **Haynesville:** no verified basin loss rate found. Gap.
- **MiQ [VERIFIED, `miq_standard_gas_v1.1.pdf`; https://miq.org/document/miq-standard-onshore/]**: Table 2 grades: A <= 0.050%, B <= 0.10%, C <= 0.20%, D <= 0.50%, E <= 1.0%, F <= 2.0%. Definition: "the quotient of the mass of methane emissions (after allocation and in kg CH4) attributed to the production of natural gas from a Facility by the mass of methane throughput sent to sales". Denominator = sales (delivered) gas, scope upstream facility, not full supply chain. Grade also requires practices and monitoring scores.
- **EPA GHGI latest / NETL:** NOT retrieved. EPA natural gas page (`epa_ngps.html`) lists 1990-2022 inventory (published April 2024) and a 1990-2023 stakeholder process "to be published April 2025"; I could not confirm a 2025 or 2026 edition or its natural gas systems CH4 total. Alvarez (2018) put the then EPA inventory ~1.4% of gross production [UNVERIFIED, press summary]. Gap.
**Definition note:** Alvarez, Zhang, Chen, Sherwin: % of gross production (Sherwin: "covered natural gas production"); EDF/MethaneSAT and MiQ: produced-and-sold/sales gas. Gross vs delivered differ by a few % relative.
**Best values:** national central 2.4% (Sherwin corrected 2.43% [2.27-2.59]) with 1.4% (EPA-ish, UNVERIFIED) low and ~3% upper; Permian ~3.1-3.6% (Bridger 3.13%/TX 3.6%; Zhang 3.7%); Appalachia ~0.5%; MiQ A 0.05%. Keep Chen 9.4% as NM Permian 2018-2020 only.
**Gaps:** EPA GHGI latest, NETL LCA, Haynesville, Varon 2023, MethaneSAT peer-reviewed Permian, midstream/transmission/distribution split (what share of loss applies to a BTM plant at wellhead/lateral vs grid plant).

## 5. Scale and technology mix of BTM gas for US data centers

**Cleanview "Bypassing the Grid" [VERIFIED, `cleanview_btm.html`; https://cleanview.co/reports/behind-the-meter-data-centers/full-report; page date not shown, data to ~May/mid-2026]**
- "59 data centers with a combined capacity of ~90 GW that plan to build their own power 'behind-the-meter.' That represents more than 25% of all planned data center capacity in the United States"; "92% ... announced since the beginning of 2025".
- Status table: Announced 33 projects 53,291 MW (60%); Permitted 16, 32,476 MW (36%); Under construction 6, 1,094 MW (1.2%); Operating 4, 1,990 MW (2.2%). Note the page text says 2.5% in one place and 2.2% in another.
- "approximately 2 GW ... online across four projects ... Colossus 1 and 2 ... 1,498 MW of operating gas turbine capacity"; Stargate Abilene 360 MW from 10 turbines (Jan 2026); Vantage Sterling VA 132 MW. Expected 2.8-3.2 GW by end 2026.
- Equipment shares by permitted/installed MW: Caterpillar 8,829 MW (33%, includes Solar Turbines 122 permitted installations 2.7 GW plus G3516/G3520K recips; Monarch WV 2 GW G3516, Joule UT G3520K); GE Vernova 8,615 (32%, TM2500, LM6000, LM2500XPRESS); Bloom 3,852 (14%); INNIO Jenbacher 2,847 (11%); ProEnergy 1,050 (4%). Top five = 94%. My rough split (not Cleanview's): recips = part of Cat + all Jenbacher, roughly 6 GW+ of 26.6 GW; turbines ~65%; fuel cells 14%. Sum of listed shares (~26.6 GW) is only ~30% of the ~90 GW, i.e. equipment is identified for a minority of capacity.
- Notable projects with prime mover (Cleanview text): xAI Colossus (mobile/aeroderivative turbines, TM2500, Solar Titan, Southaven MS); Stargate Abilene (Crusoe; LM2500XPRESS + Solar Titan 350); Stargate Frontier TX (VoltaGrid, 620 Jenbacher J624/J620 engines, 2.58 GW; Oracle 2.3 GW VoltaGrid deal); Stargate Jupiter NM (2.45 GW Bloom fuel cells replacing gas turbines, April 2026); Meta Socrates N/S New Albany OH (Williams, 2x200 MW, Solar turbines); EdgeConneX New Albany (Jenbacher, 32 engines/126 MW + 216 MW phase 2); Meta El Paso (Enchanted Rock, 366 MW, 813 x 450 kW Generac units); Crusoe Goodnight TX (20 LM6000, 933 MW, with ProEnergy); Crusoe Jade WY (900 MW Bloom + ~1.8 GW turbines); Monarch WV (2 GW Cat G3516 recips); Joule UT (Cat G3520K, 4 GW deal); Nebius Vineland NJ (400 MW Bergen recips, possibly Bloom); Homer City PA (4.5 GW).
- Earlier edition (Feb 2026): "46 projects / 56 GW, ~30%" seen only in search-summary [UNVERIFIED]. Another AFP-shared figure "~60 data centers / 97 GW" [UNVERIFIED].
**xAI Southaven permit [UNVERIFIED, search summary of Mississippi Free Press / WDAM]:** MS Permit Board approved 41 turbines on 10 Mar 2026 (xAI cites 1.2 GW), replacing 27 unpermitted turbines; Colossus 1 had up to 35 turbines. Not opened.
**Meta Hyperion LA [UNVERIFIED, search summary]:** NOT behind-the-meter; Entergy Louisiana utility CCGTs (Franklin Farms 1,500 MW, 2 units in Richland Parish; Waterford "Selpa" 754 MW; 2.26 GW approved 20 Aug 2025), later +7 plants/5.2 GW (March 2026). Treat as grid-supplied (marginal) gas CC, not BTM.
**Best values:** ~90 GW planned BTM, ~2 GW operating mid-2026, ~3 GW by end 2026; operating fleet is almost entirely turbines (xAI 1.5 GW + Abilene 0.36 GW), planned fleet heavy in recips (Cat, Jenbacher) and Bloom. Operating 2026 mix is therefore not representative of the planned mix.
**Gaps:** Enverus counts; per-project engine type confirmations from permits; Cleanview report publication date; Bloom fuel cell CH4/CO2 per kWh.
