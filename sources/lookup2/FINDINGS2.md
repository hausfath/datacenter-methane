Quote: "Total CH4 emissions in 2023 were estimated to be 1,623 kt (45.4 MMT CO2 Eq.)". Table 3-36 (kt CH4, 2023): UG mining net 1,193 (liberated 2,122, recovered&used -928); surface 211; post-mining UG 172; post-mining surface 46; total 1,623.
Abandoned (Table 3-41, 2023): gross 323 kt, recovered&used -104, net 219 kt (6.1 MMT CO2e). Separate from the 1,623.
EIA (Monthly Energy Review CSV, https://www.eia.gov/totalenergy/data/browser/csv.php?tbl=T01.03 and T02.06; saved eia_mer_*.csv), 2023: total coal consumption 8.170788 quads; electric power 7,247.322 trillion Btu (387,205 kst); total 425,895 kst.
Arithmetic (2023): 1,623e6 kg / 8.170788e9 MMBtu = 0.199 kg CH4/MMBtu (all coal). Electric-power-only denominator: 1,623e6/7.247e9 = 0.224 (overstates if mine CH4 is allocated to power only). Abandoned net adds 0.027 (gross 0.040). Active+abandoned net = 0.226 kg/MMBtu all-coal basis. CO2e (GWP28): 5.6 kg CO2e/MMBtu. Caveats: US production (524 Mst) exceeds consumption (426 Mst; exports), so a consumption-based factor is an upper bound; mine CH4 is mostly Appalachian/Illinois UG, not average coal.

## 3. Transmission-only loss
EIA FAQ 105 (https://www.eia.gov/tools/faqs/faq.php?id=105&t=3, V): "annual electricity transmission and distribution (T&D) losses averaged about 5% ... in 2018 through 2022" (combined; EIA gives no transmission-only number). DOE QER analysis (qer_td_losses.pdf, V): combined "about 6%"; NY study "transmission losses ranged from 1.5 to 5.8%"; an Australian analysis 1.1-2.9%. A US transmission-only figure of ~2-3% (older GAO "about 2.5 percent") is U/dated. Best defensible: transmission roughly 2% (range 1-3%) of generation, flagged as not from a current EIA/lab primary source.
