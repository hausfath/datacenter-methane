## Functional unit and accounting
The functional unit is 1 MWh net delivered to the data-center bus. Emissions are life-cycle for the fuel only: combustion, supply-chain CO2, engine methane slip, upstream methane, and
N2O. Embodied plant emissions, water and local air pollution are out of scope.

E = HR × [53.06·(1 + f) + GWP·(m_slip + m_up + m_fac) + 273·1e-4] / (1 − loss)

| Symbol | Meaning | Value and source |
|---|---|---|
| HR | heat rate, MMBtu HHV/MWh | §Cases |
| 53.06 | combustion CO2, kg/MMBtu | 40 CFR 98 Table C-1 |
| f | supply-chain CO2 (processing, compressor fuel, flaring) as a share of combustion CO2 | 0.093, set from NETL Rev1. The upstream total of 7.8 g CO2e/MJ minus its 21.0 kg/MWh CH4 term, divided by 337.6 kg/MWh combustion |
| m_up | upstream CH4, kg/MMBtu delivered | c_CH4·L/(1−L), with c_CH4 = 18.7 kg CH4/MMBtu (NETL 128 kg NG/MWh at 53.6% HHV → 20.1 kg NG/MMBtu × CH4 mass fraction 0.93, range 0.884–0.970). L is CH4 emitted per CH4 in gross production, whole chain |
| m_fac | on-site leaks | break-even only |
| loss | electrical losses | BTM 1% (on-site MV and parasitic); grid 3% (transmission). No primary transmission-only figure was found. EIA gives ~5% total T&D, and 2% and 5% are run as sensitivities |

**V1.** Using NETL's own inputs (53.6% HHV, 0.55% kg CH4/kg NG delivered ÷ 0.93):

| Component | This model | NETL |
|---|---|---|
| Combustion CO2 | 337.8 | 337.6 |
| Upstream CH4 term | 21.1 | 21.0 |
| Total, generated basis | 391.2 | 394.7 (−0.9%) |

All three pass. The upstream-CO2 factor is calibrated to NETL, so it is not an independent check. The NETL fleet peaker (659.7) is reproduced only to within 5–8%. We think the cause is its CF < 0.2
basis, but have not verified this, and the peaker is not used as a check.

## Decision rule (pre-registered)
- **Primary cells:** GWP100 (AR6 fossil CH4 29.8) × L = 2.3% × {ERCOT, PJM, US} × {A, B}.
- **CO2-only state:** strips CH4 from both sides at once.
- **Tie band:** 5%, widened to the half-width of the within-cell Monte Carlo 90% interval wherever that is larger (5.3–20%).
- **Monte Carlo:** draws are correlated and shared by both sides, N = 20,000:

  | Parameter | Distribution |
  |---|---|
  | L | Normal(2.3%, 0.35%): a judgment spread, wider than the Alvarez 95% CI (+0.4/−0.3, sd ≈ 0.18%); the extra width widens tie bands, which works in BTM's favor |
  | CH4 mass fraction | U(0.884, 0.970) |
  | f | U(0.08, 0.11) |
  | GWP100 CH4 | Normal(29.8, 6.7) |
  | Grid loss | U(2%, 5%) |
  | BTM heat rate | uniform over the case range |
  | Recip slip | U(0.24, 0.65) |

## V3 and FaIR
V3 is a partial pass. On an SSP2-4.5 2030 background with Thornhill2021 CH4 lifetime, FaIR's 100-year integrated-forcing ratio for CH4 is 37.2 (5–95%: 29–47). AR6 gives 27.0 ± 11 for
non-fossil CH4 (from memory, verify). The temperature ratio at 100 years is 8.0 (3.2–15.2), against AR6 GTP100 4.7.
- The ozone forcing per CH4 pulse is 0.61× the direct forcing.
- The perturbation e-folding time is 13.2 years.
- CO2 AGWP100 is 7.95e-14 W m-2 yr kg-1, against AR6's 8.95e-14.

FaIR therefore weights methane more heavily than GWP100 does, which works against BTM. FaIR results are reported as a complement only.

## Caveats
- **Grid vintage.** eGRID is 2023. The grid serving 2027+ load may be cleaner or dirtier, and holding it fixed favors BTM if the grid decarbonizes.
- **Bridge and backup power.** BTM plants often serve as bridge power until the grid connection arrives, or later become backup. Their lifetime emissions depend on hours run, which is not modelled here.
- **Redundancy.** The N+1 and part-load penalty (+3–5% HR, slip ×1.0–1.3) applies to islanded plants only and is not in the central case. Including it would raise recip and turbine totals.
- **Permian.** It is oil-driven, so the marginal molecule for new gas demand may come from the Haynesville. The main analysis has no Haynesville case; the regional section uses Haynesville 0.7% (0.4–1.0%) for Memphis.
- **Measurement gaps.** No CH4 measurement exists for Bloom SOFC exhaust (0 assumed). No aeroderivative slip measurements were found (W-7 turbine factor used).
- **Cambium.** LRMER is pre-OBBBA and, by NREL's own guidance, not for 2020s conditions. It is shown for context only.

## Setup
- **Regions.** Seven hotspot regions, taken from Cleanview project locations. Each is paired with its supplying basin's leak rate, matched on both sides. Leak rates are in `data/basin_leak_rates.json`, compiled in `sources/basins/BASINS.md`. Sources:
  - MacKay et al. 2026, ACP (MethaneAIR, 12 basins)
  - Varon et al. 2026, ES&T (TROPOMI)
  - Bridger 2024 inventory
  - AMI 2026
  - Sherwin et al. 2024, corrected SI
- **Grid A.** The EIA-930 consumption-based CO2 intensity of each balancing authority, which allocates interchange with an MRIO model (per-BA workbooks). The year is 2025, except SPP, where 2025 lacks December and 2024 is used. Added to it:
  - upstream gas CH4 and supply-chain CO2;
  - regional coal-mine CH4: surface/PRB/lignite mines 0.04 and underground mines 0.26 kg CH4/MMBtu (EPA GHGI 2023 Table 3-36; EIA ACR 2023 Table 1);
  - coal supply-chain CO2 at 2%;
  - the total is then divided by 0.97 (3% transmission loss).
- **Grid B.** A new H-class NGCC burning local gas.
- **States.** Four states with per-region MC tie bands (N = 20,000; L drawn triangular over the basin range).

## Latent issues in the main analysis found by the regional review
These are flagged only. The main numbers are unchanged.
1. **Coal-mine CH4 factor.** The main analysis uses the national factor (0.199) everywhere. ERCOT's coal is surface lignite and PRB, at about 0.04. The national factor adds about 7 kg/MWh to ERCOT's grid (1.7% of A), which favors BTM.
2. **Coal supply-chain CO2.** It is missing on the grid side, about −3 kg/MWh in ERCOT and PJM, which favors the grid.

The two partly offset, and neither changes a main verdict.

## Caveats
- **Upstream scaling.** The consumption basis scales the grid's upstream terms by the BA's own fuel mix, which assumes imports have the same mix.
- **CO2 only.** EIA-930 reports CO2 only, so combustion CH4 and N2O are omitted on the grid side (<0.5%).
- **Basin rates.**
  - Denominators are not harmonized (gross, dry, covered or marketed gas).
  - Rockies values are low confidence.
  - Powder River and San Juan have no verified rate.
- **Cambium.** The 2024 LRMER for each site's GEA region is in `results/regional/regional_cases.csv` as pre-OBBBA context only.

## Short-run marginal rate (M1)
- **Data.**
  - EPA CAMPD Part 75 hourly unit data (quarterly bulk files, streamed and aggregated to BA × fuel × unit class × UTC hour).
  - EIA-930 hourly "Demand (Adjusted)".
  - Fuel is classified per unit-hour by CO2/heat input (<60 gas, 60–85 oil, ≥85 kg/MMBtu coal).
  - Plant→BA comes from eGRID2023, falling back to EIA-860M (Sep 2026) Operating and Retired. Unmapped CO2 is ≤0.4 Mt per quarter.
  - Part 75 contains no reciprocating engines and few units under 25 MW, so a marginal recip fleet on the grid side is missing. This biases the grid rate low.
- **Primary spec (after the change-log rule):** levels, CO2e_t = β·D_t + γ·(wind_t, solar_t) + month FE + hour-of-day FE, 2025.
  - Boundaries:
    - ERCOT: ERCOT CEMS on ERCO demand.
    - PJM: PJM-own CEMS on PJM demand. The joint Eastern-Interconnection boundary failed the plausibility check: fossil share 1.24, and the PJM and rest-of-EI demand series correlate at 0.88 after fixed effects.
    - US: load-weighted mean of the TX, Eastern and Western interconnection estimates.
  - Delivered = β/(1 − 0.06), using marginal losses. At 3% average losses the values are 521 / 745 / 638.
  - 90% block-bootstrap CI: 420–632 / 729–785 / 632–669.
- **Pre-registered spec.** Without wind and solar controls, ERCOT = 383 (fossil share 0.55) and PJM joint = 1,053 (fossil share 1.24). Both failed D1e. The US value of 622 passed.
- **Diagnostics:**
  - **D1a timestamps.** CEMS gross load vs EIA-930 fossil generation peaks at lag 0 in every month for ERCOT in 2023 and 2025 (r = 0.99). PJM peaks at lag 0 in 2025.
    - PJM's EIA-930 fuel-mix columns run 1 h off in 2019 and 2023 and are shifted to align; PJM demand is aligned.
    - The pre-registered demand-vs-CO2 lag test peaks at +1 to +2 h in ERCOT from spring through fall. This is physical: batteries and solar move the net-load ramp.
  - **D1b, Holland reproduction** (full-year 2019, no controls, interconnection demand):
    - Texas 482 vs 492 kg CO2/MWh, −2.1% (NW SE 15);
    - Eastern 621 vs 638, −2.7% (NW SE 8).
    - Pass. An earlier January–September run gave 484 and 614.
  - **D1c, AVERT v4.3 2023** at generation level (Texas 560, Mid-Atlantic 574, national about 600):
    - Fossil-load variant: −7.7%, +8.5% and −0.8%. Pass.
    - Demand spec: −23.5%, −6.7% and −18.8%, outside the pre-registered 5–15%-below expectation for ERCOT and the US.
  - **D1d, fuel closure:** +1.2% / −0.8% / 0.0%. These values come after all fuel equations were moved to a shared outlier-screened sample; the first run missed PJM by −3.7% (change log).
  - **D1e, plausibility (primary):**
    - fossil share 0.79 / 0.93 / 0.85;
    - marginal gas heat rate 8.0 / 9.3 / 8.7 MMBtu/MWh.
- **Storage (methods review SF2).** Hourly estimators count battery discharge as zero-emission. In ERCOT the daily-total spec with controls, which nets intra-day storage, gives 663 against the primary's 538.
  - At 663, engines and turbines would tie the short-run margin in ERCOT too (1.06–1.11×).
  - The ERCOT short-run verdict is therefore sensitive to storage treatment. The figure subtitle reports ratio ranges rather than per-region verdicts.
- **Not done.**
  - 2024: the EPA DEMO_KEY rate limit blocked it. (2019 Q4 arrived later, and D1b was rerun on the full year.)
  - Substitute-data screening: no indicators were kept.
  - Regional coal-mine CH4: 0.199 is used throughout, which raises the grid rate.

## Long-run marginal rate (M2, Cambium 2024)
- **Matched reconstruction.** Gas and coal heat input are backed out of Cambium's combustion CO2 and CH4 (53.06 G + 95.52 C; 1.0 G + 11.0 C g), and the project's upstream accounting is then applied.
  - Check: the reconstruction reproduces Cambium's precombustion CO2 within 2.9% (ERCOT Mid-case) and ≤1.6% in the other cases.
  - The matched values sit within about 2 kg of native.
- **Levelization.** It reproduces the workbook's cached ERCOT, PJM East and PJM West values to 1e-12.
- **Central choices.** High Demand Growth, 2027–2046, 3% discount (Cambium default), end-use → busbar (3.6% distribution loss) then /0.97.
  - US = GEA regions weighted by county population (Census 2024 estimates; 10 counties unmatched).
  - PJM = PJM_East, where Loudoun County sits. The population-weighted East/West value is 271.
- **Scenario range (3%):** ERCOT 140–175, PJM_East 200–306, US 166–237. The simple means (0%) are 165 / 276 / 199.
- **Bias.** All Cambium 2024 scenarios keep IRA credits without phase-out and include the 2024 EPA §111 rule. EPA partially repealed that rule in Federal Register doc. 2026-19071, "Partial Repeal of the Carbon Pollution Standards for Fossil Fuel-Fired Electric Generating Units", published 2026-09-17, effective 2026-11-16 per law-firm alerts. Both points make the long-run rate too low by an unknown amount.

## Blend and framing conventions
- The blend follows Gagnon & O'Shaughnessy 2024 (NREL/TP-6A40-91580): MER = w·SRMER + (1 − w)·LRMER. Their worked example (a 3-year lag on a 20-year load) gives w = 0.15. We use w = 0.25 (a 5-year lag) with a range of 0.15–0.35. **The report was funded by Amazon**; cite it with that disclosure.
- **Concession.** For crediting, the CDM grid-emission-factor convention (Tool 07) appears to assign projects that *increase* grid consumption the operating margin only (w_OM = 1, w_BM = 0). On that convention the short-run rate is the relevant comparator.
  - This comes from Verra's Oct 2024 draft methodology text; the UNFCCC TOOL07 text itself was not read.
  - It is a crediting rule, not a consequential estimate.
- Holland et al. (reply to Gagnon et al., PNAS 2022) describe their estimates as short-run and say "a range of possible impacts is most informative". The quote was read by the domain reviewer, not re-checked here. That supports showing both horizons.

## Build margins (M3, M4)
- **Units.** Units are plant × prime-mover groups (CC parts merged). Data: EIA-860M Sep 2026 and EIA-923 2025 Final (Page 1, fuel for electricity). Storage is excluded. Gas CO2e uses `em.intensity()` with W-7 slip by prime mover.
- **Recent additions.**
  - Tool 07 rule (as quoted in the Verra Oct 2024 draft): the newest groups with a full year of 2025 data that together supply ≥20% of 2025 regional generation.
    - ERCOT reaches back to 2018 (3% gas by MWh).
    - PJM reaches back to 2017 (81% gas, the CC wave).
  - Mixed-vintage groups (>3 years between units) are excluded: 50 / 147 / 1,321.
- **Planned additions.** Nameplate × region-technology capacity factor from 2020–24 units, with national fallbacks; nuclear CF 0.92.
  - ERCOT all statuses: 58 GW, of which 17 GW is gas. Under construction: 20 GW, 3.9 GW gas.
  - PJM under construction is almost entirely solar and wind (1 kg/MWh); all statuses give 231.
- **Caveats.** These are averages of what is being built, not what a new load induces. Matching is on annual energy, with no night-time firming.
