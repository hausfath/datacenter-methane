## Functional unit and accounting
The functional unit is 1 MWh net delivered to the data-center bus. Emissions are life-cycle for the fuel only: combustion, supply-chain CO2, engine methane slip, upstream methane, and
N2O. Embodied plant emissions, water and local air pollution are out of scope.

E = HR × [53.06·(1 + f) + GWP·(m_slip + m_up + m_fac) + 273·1e-4] / (1 − loss)

| Symbol | Meaning | Value and source |
|---|---|---|
| HR | heat rate, MMBtu HHV/MWh | §Cases |
| 53.06 | combustion CO2, kg/MMBtu | 40 CFR 98 Table C-1 |
| f | supply-chain CO2 (processing, compressor fuel, flaring) as a share of combustion CO2 | 0.093, set from NETL Rev1. The upstream total of 7.8 g CO2e/MJ minus its 21.0 kg/MWh CH4 term, divided by 337.6 kg/MWh combustion |
| m_up | upstream CH4, kg/MMBtu delivered | c_CH4·L/(1−L), with c_CH4 = 18.7 kg CH4/MMBtu (NETL 128 kg NG/MWh at 53.6% HHV → 20.1 kg NG/MMBtu × CH4 mass fraction 0.93, range 0.884–0.970). L is CH4 emitted per CH4 in gross production, whole chain |
| m_fac | on-site leaks | break-even only |
| loss | electrical losses | BTM 1% (on-site MV and parasitic); grid 3% (transmission). No primary transmission-only figure was found. EIA gives ~5% total T&D, and 2% and 5% are run as sensitivities |

**V1.** Using NETL's own inputs (53.6% HHV, 0.55% kg CH4/kg NG delivered ÷ 0.93):

| Component | This model | NETL |
|---|---|---|
| Combustion CO2 | 337.8 | 337.6 |
| Upstream CH4 term | 21.1 | 21.0 |
| Total, generated basis | 391.2 | 394.7 (−0.9%) |

All three pass. The upstream-CO2 factor is calibrated to NETL, so it is not an independent check. The NETL fleet peaker (659.7) is reproduced only to within 5–8%. We think the cause is its CF < 0.2
basis, but have not verified this, and the peaker is not used as a check.

Lease and plant fuel and NGL removal are ignored; they change the conversion by a few percent, relative. These are whole-basin rates with no oil/gas allocation. Energy allocation would cut a ~3.2%

## Grid references
eGRID2023 rev2 (released 6/12/2025; still the latest on 24 Oct 2026). Balancing authorities ERCO and PJM, plus the US total. Gas and coal fuel per grid MWh =
generation share × (CO2 output rate ÷ CO2 input rate) for each fuel.

| Region | eGRID CO2e, kg/MWh | Gas MMBtu/MWh | Coal MMBtu/MWh |
|---|---|---|---|
| ERCOT | 334.2 | 3.63 | 1.43 |
| PJM | 326.3 | 3.34 | 1.50 |
| US | 349.7 | 3.27 | 1.74 |

- **(A) Average.** eGRID CO2e, plus gas supply CO2 (f), plus upstream CH4 at the matched L, plus coal-mine CH4, all divided by (1 − 0.03).
  - Coal-mine CH4 = 0.199 kg per MMBtu of coal: EPA GHGI 1990–2023 (EPA 430-R-25-003; 1,623 kt in 2023) ÷ EIA 2023 coal consumption (8.17 quads). The EPA copy read was hosted by EDF.
  - Including abandoned mines gives 0.226, which moves the grid by about 1 kg/MWh.
- **(B) New grid NGCC.** The same gas and L as the BTM case, divided by (1 − 0.03).
- **Ranking test.** BTM counts as worse only if it exceeds both (A) and (B).
- **Context only: Cambium 2024 LRMER.** Combustion plus precombustion, 2027–2046 simple mean, AR6 GWP100, end-use basis. Its policies date from August 2024, before OBBBA.

## Decision rule (pre-registered)
- **Primary cells:** GWP100 (AR6 fossil CH4 29.8) × L = 2.3% × {ERCOT, PJM, US} × {A, B}.
- **CO2-only state:** strips CH4 from both sides at once.
- **Tie band:** 5%, widened to the half-width of the within-cell Monte Carlo 90% interval wherever that is larger (5.3–20%).
- **Monte Carlo:** draws are correlated and shared by both sides, N = 20,000:

  | Parameter | Distribution |
  |---|---|
  | L | Normal(2.3%, 0.35%) |
  | CH4 mass fraction | U(0.884, 0.970) |
  | f | U(0.08, 0.11) |
  | GWP100 CH4 | Normal(29.8, 6.7) |
  | Grid loss | U(2%, 5%) |
  | BTM heat rate | uniform over the case range |
  | Recip slip | U(0.24, 0.65) |

## V2: Frost Methane's fleet numbers
- A pure-CH4 mass balance, with 7.49% of produced CH4 emitted and the rest burned, reproduces 724/380 to four figures (1.9057 vs 1.9053), using AR5 GWPs 28/84 as Irzak states them.
  The implied CH4 share at GWP100 is 45%, inside her stated 40–50%.
- The total pins only CF × HR × duration:
  - read as 18 months of operation, CF × HR ≈ 6.07 MMBtu/MWh (about 253 Mt/yr);
  - read as an annual rate, CF × HR ≈ 9.1.
- Splitting the 7.5% between slip and leaks before the engine depends on the fleet mix:
  - an all-recip fleet at 2.8% slip leaves ~4.8% before the engine, consistent with her 4% Permian figure plus ~0.8% facility leaks;
  - a ~35% recip fleet (Cleanview equipment shares) leaves ~6.6%.

  The split cannot be identified from the episode.
- About 2 GW of behind-the-meter capacity is operating, against the 50 GW assumed (Cleanview).

## V3 and FaIR
V3 is a partial pass. On an SSP2-4.5 2030 background with Thornhill2021 CH4 lifetime, FaIR's 100-year integrated-forcing ratio for CH4 is 37.2 (5–95%: 29–47). AR6 gives 27.0 ± 11 for
non-fossil CH4 (from memory, verify). The temperature ratio at 100 years is 8.0 (3.2–15.2), against AR6 GTP100 4.7.
- The ozone forcing per CH4 pulse is 0.61× the direct forcing.
- The perturbation e-folding time is 13.2 years.
- CO2 AGWP100 is 7.95e-14 W m-2 yr kg-1, against AR6's 8.95e-14.

FaIR therefore weights methane more heavily than GWP100 does, which works against BTM. FaIR results are reported as a complement only.

Background dependence is small. The recip/ERCOT ratio in 2050 is 2.36–2.39 across SSP1-2.6, SSP2-4.5 and SSP3-7.0, and the SOFC/ERCOT ratio is 1.19–1.19.

Because FaIR's CH4 metric runs about 38% above AR6 GWP100 on this background (37.2 vs 27.0) (V3), the mid-century ratios lean against BTM. They should be quoted as "in FaIR", not as AR6-consistent.

## Caveats
- **Grid vintage.** eGRID is 2023. The grid serving 2027+ load may be cleaner or dirtier, and holding it fixed favors BTM if the grid decarbonizes.
- **Bridge and backup power.** BTM plants often serve as bridge power until the grid connection arrives, or later become backup. Their lifetime emissions depend on hours run, which is not modelled here.
- **Redundancy.** The N+1 and part-load penalty (+3–5% HR, slip ×1.0–1.3) applies to islanded plants only and is not in the central case. Including it would raise recip and turbine totals.
- **Permian.** It is oil-driven, so the marginal molecule for new gas demand may come from the Haynesville. No verified Haynesville loss rate was found.
- **Measurement gaps.** No CH4 measurement exists for Bloom SOFC exhaust (0 assumed). No aeroderivative slip measurements were found (W-7 turbine factor used).
- **Cambium.** LRMER is pre-OBBBA and, by NREL's own guidance, not for 2020s conditions. It is shown for context only.
